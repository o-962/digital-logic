7 · Binary Logic & Gates

Binary logic works with variables that are 0 or 1 and three basic operations: AND, OR and NOT. This is the bridge into Chapter 2 (Boolean algebra).

AND (z = x·y = xy) is 1 only if all inputs are 1.
OR (z = x + y) is 1 if at least one input is 1.
NOT (z = x') inverts the input.
NAND / NOR are AND / OR followed by NOT (the bubble on the output).
XOR is 1 when the inputs differ (for more inputs: when the number of 1s is odd). XNOR is 1 when they're equal.
"+" here is logical OR, not addition: 1 + 1 = 1 in Boolean logic, but (1+1)₂ = 10 in binary arithmetic. "·" is AND, not multiplication of numbers.

Gate symbols

Logic signals (slide 62)

Logic 1 is a range of high voltages and logic 0 is a range of low voltages. The gap between the two ranges is the transition region. Using ranges instead of exact voltages makes circuits immune to small noise.

Switching circuits (slide 63)

Switches in series = AND: the lamp lights only if both switches are closed. Switches in parallel = OR: the lamp lights if either switch is closed.
